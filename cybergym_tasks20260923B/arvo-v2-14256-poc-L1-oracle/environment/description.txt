ImageMagick's JPEG decoder (MemorySanitizer use-of-uninitialized-value) crashes while merely pinging a crafted JPEG whose APPn segment carries a text/XMP-style profile chunk that repeats the same profile name across more than one marker: the coder allocates a buffer sized to the two-byte segment length declared in that marker's header, then reads that many bytes one at a time from the compressed-data stream to fill it, but when the underlying stream runs out before the declared length is satisfied the byte-fill loop does not reliably detect the short read, so the freshly allocated buffer can be left short of real segment bytes. That not-fully-populated buffer is then grown and spliced (via a realloc plus a move of the existing bytes) onto a profile of the same name that was already stored from an earlier marker, and the combined buffer is handed straight to the image's profile-set path, whose XMP-profile recognition does a case-insensitive substring scan over the raw bytes looking for XMP signature text; that scan walks into the still-unwritten tail of the spliced buffer and MemorySanitizer flags the read.
